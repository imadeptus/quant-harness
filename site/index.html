The problem

Why most backtests lie

Not through fraud — through the default way a backtest is written. Every one of these is easy to do by accident, and each one alone is enough to turn noise into a Sharpe ratio worth pitching.

01

Look-ahead bias

The strategy peeks at information from bar close that wasn't actually available when the position was opened.

02

In-sample scoring

Training and evaluating on the same window. The equity curve you're shown was fit to, not tested against.

03

Costs added later, or not at all

Taker fees, slippage and funding are the difference between a gross edge and a net one — and they're often missing.

04

Multiple testing, uncorrected

Try a few hundred configurations and show the best. The winner's Sharpe is inflated by the search itself, not by edge.

05

Survivorship

Delisted pairs, dead exchanges and abandoned parameter sets quietly disappear from the sample that gets reported.

What you get

A mechanical PASS/KILL verdict, not an opinion

Every submission runs through the same calibrated judge: Combinatorial Purged CV builds honest out-of-sample paths, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio corrects for the number of trials, PBO reports whether the search overfit, and a drawdown and trade-count gate keep the verdict grounded.

  • Checks table — trade count, OOS Sharpe, max drawdown, DSR, each against a pre-registered threshold.
  • Path Sharpes — median and worst-path Sharpe across every CPCV split, not one lucky curve.
  • DSR with trial count — the Sharpe deflated by how many configurations were actually tried.
  • PBO — probability of backtest overfitting, reported for context (it never overrides the verdict).
  • Cost sensitivity — the verdict re-run at 0x / 0.5x / 1x / 2x your stated cost, so "profitable at zero fees" is visible for what it is.
  • A reproducible command — the exact invocation that produced the report, so anyone can re-run it on the same data.

Calibration

The judge was measured, not just trusted

A "reject everything" tool would look identical to a calibrated one until you check it against known ground truth. It was checked: 200 Monte-Carlo runs per cell, synthetic data with a known planted edge, mapping the full PASS/KILL response surface.

0% PASS rate on pure noise (N=6 configs) — the judge does not pass noise
~2.2 annualized Sharpe where detection power crosses 50% PASS
≤1% false-positive rate at zero edge under fat tails (0%) and autocorrelation up to φ=0.6 (worst cell 0.5%)
10 / 3 pre-registered hypotheses tested across exchanges, all rejected

Full methodology and every calibration table: reports/CALIBRATION.md in the repository. This does not track live returns as a product — an internal research paper-tracker exists purely for methodology testing and is not part of what an audit reports on.

FAQ

Questions worth answering upfront

What if my strategy fails?

You get a KILL verdict and the report explaining exactly which gate failed — trade count, OOS Sharpe, drawdown or the Deflated Sharpe threshold — with the numbers behind it. A KILL is a real result, not a rejected submission; nothing stops you from re-testing a revised strategy.

Do you see my code?

No. The audit runs on a returns CSV (and optionally a trades CSV) — the outcome of your strategy, not its logic. Your signal, code and parameters never need to leave your environment.

Which markets does this work for?

Any market where you can produce a per-period returns series — crypto spot and perpetuals, equities, futures. The judge is asset-agnostic; it only needs the returns, the bar frequency, and how many configurations you tried.

What's the turnaround?

48 hours for a submitted audit. The API returns a verdict synchronously, in the time one CPCV run takes on your data.

Is this investment advice?

No. This is statistical software and a report on the data you supply — a screen against pre-registered thresholds, not a recommendation to trade, allocate to, or avoid any strategy, asset or manager. See the disclaimer below.

Refunds?

If the input can't be judged at all — malformed data, insufficient periods, an input error the CLI itself would reject — that submission is refunded or re-run for free. A KILL verdict on valid data is a delivered result, not a refund case: the judge did its job.
